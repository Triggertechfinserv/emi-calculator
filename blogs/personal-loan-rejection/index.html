Top 10 Reasons Why Personal Loan Applications Get Rejected in India (2026 Guide)

Updated on July 7, 2026 • By TechFinserv Team • 12 min read

Applying for a personal loan has become easier than ever. With digital KYC, instant eligibility checks, and same-day disbursals, many borrowers expect quick approval. However, thousands of personal loan applications are rejected every day because applicants fail to meet a lender's eligibility criteria.

A personal loan rejection doesn't always mean you have poor finances. Banks and NBFCs evaluate several factors before approving a loan, including your credit score, monthly income, repayment capacity, employment stability, debt obligations, and previous repayment history.

Understanding these reasons before applying can significantly improve your approval chances, reduce unnecessary hard enquiries on your credit report, and help you secure better interest rates.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll explain the top reasons why personal loan applications get rejected, how lenders assess risk, and practical steps you can take to increase your chances of getting approved.

Top 10 Reasons Why Personal Loan Applications Get Rejected

Most personal loan rejections happen because borrowers overlook important eligibility requirements before applying. Understanding these common reasons can help you avoid rejection and improve your approval chances.

1. Low CIBIL Score or Poor Credit History

Your CIBIL score is one of the first things banks check after receiving your loan application. It reflects your repayment behaviour and helps lenders assess the risk of lending money to you.

Most banks prefer applicants with a credit score above 750. While some NBFCs may approve loans for borrowers with lower scores, the interest rate is usually higher and the approved loan amount may be lower.

A low credit score usually indicates previous financial difficulties or poor repayment discipline. Common reasons include missed EMI payments, credit card defaults, loan settlements, frequent loan enquiries, and high credit utilisation.

Common reasons your CIBIL score becomes low

  • Late EMI payments
  • Credit card bill defaults
  • Loan settlement instead of full closure
  • Using more than 30–40% of your credit limit
  • Multiple loan enquiries within a short period
  • Writing off loans

Expert Tip: Before applying for a personal loan, check your credit report for errors. Incorrect loan reporting or delayed updates can unnecessarily reduce your score.

2. High FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio)

FOIR is another major reason why banks reject personal loan applications. It measures how much of your monthly income is already committed towards existing loan repayments.

If a large portion of your salary is already being used to pay EMIs, banks assume you may struggle to repay another loan.

The formula used is:

FOIR = (Total Monthly EMIs ÷ Net Monthly Income) × 100

Example

  • Monthly Salary = ₹60,000
  • Current EMIs = ₹38,000

FOIR = 63%

Although some lenders allow FOIR up to 70%, many banks prefer applicants below 50–60%, especially for unsecured personal loans.

FOIR Loan Approval
Below 40% Excellent
40%–50% Good
50%–65% Average
Above 65% High Rejection Risk

To improve your FOIR before applying:

  • Close small personal loans
  • Pay off credit card dues
  • Avoid buying products on EMI
  • Increase your monthly income if possible
  • Add a co-applicant where permitted

3. Insufficient Monthly Income

Every bank specifies a minimum monthly income requirement based on the applicant's city, employer category, and loan amount requested.

For example, if your salary is ₹22,000 but you apply for a ₹20 lakh personal loan, the lender may reject the application because your repayment capacity doesn't match the requested amount.

Banks don't approve loans simply because an applicant has a job. They evaluate whether the borrower can comfortably pay the EMI after meeting household expenses.

Income factors banks consider

  • Net monthly salary
  • Employer category
  • Job stability
  • Future repayment capacity
  • Existing obligations
  • Annual income growth

Higher salaries usually qualify for:

  • Higher loan amount
  • Lower interest rates
  • Longer repayment tenure
  • Faster approvals

4. Too Many Existing Loans

Having multiple active loans is another major reason why banks reject personal loan applications.

Even if you repay all EMIs on time, lenders become cautious when they see several active loans because your financial obligations are already high.

For example:

  • Home Loan
  • Car Loan
  • Two Personal Loans
  • Consumer Durable EMI
  • Credit Card EMI
  • Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL)

Although each EMI may appear manageable individually, together they significantly increase your repayment burden.

Banks usually evaluate both:

  • Total outstanding loan amount
  • Total monthly EMI commitment

Applicants with fewer active loans generally have a much better chance of approval.

5. Unstable Employment or Frequent Job Changes

Banks prefer applicants with a stable source of income. If you've changed jobs multiple times in the last one or two years, lenders may consider your income unstable and reject your application.

Most banks require salaried applicants to have:

  • At least 6 months with the current employer
  • 1–2 years of total work experience
  • Regular salary credits in the bank account

For self-employed applicants, lenders generally expect:

  • Business continuity of at least 2–3 years
  • Consistent income
  • Stable GST or ITR records

Why employment stability matters

  • Shows consistent income
  • Reduces repayment risk
  • Improves confidence in long-term repayment ability
  • Increases eligibility for higher loan amounts

Expert Tip: If you've recently switched jobs, consider waiting until you've completed your probation period before applying for a personal loan.

6. Incorrect or Incomplete Loan Application

Many loan applications are rejected simply because applicants make mistakes while filling out the form.

Even small errors can delay verification or result in outright rejection.

Common application mistakes

  • Wrong mobile number
  • Incorrect PAN or Aadhaar number
  • Salary mismatch
  • Incorrect employer details
  • Missing address information
  • Typing errors in income details
  • Incomplete bank account information

Banks verify every detail through KYC documents, employer verification, income proof, and credit bureau records. Any mismatch raises concerns about the authenticity of the application.

Application Error Possible Impact
Wrong PAN Number KYC Failure
Salary mismatch Income verification failure
Incorrect employer Employment verification issue
Address mismatch Additional verification
Missing documents Application rejection

7. Applying to Multiple Banks at the Same Time

Many borrowers believe applying to several banks increases their chances of approval. In reality, it often has the opposite effect.

Every loan application usually creates a hard enquiry on your credit report. Multiple enquiries within a short period suggest that you urgently need funds or that other lenders have declined your application.

This may reduce your credit score and increase the perceived lending risk.

Best Practice

  • Check your eligibility first.
  • Compare interest rates.
  • Apply only with lenders where you meet the eligibility criteria.
  • Avoid submitting multiple applications within a few weeks.

A single well-planned application has a much higher success rate than applying everywhere at once.

8. Limited or No Credit History

A person with no credit history may also face rejection.

While having no loans may seem like a positive thing, banks have no previous repayment record to evaluate your financial discipline.

This is commonly known as being a New-to-Credit (NTC) applicant.

How to build a credit history

  • Use a credit card responsibly.
  • Pay every EMI before the due date.
  • Maintain low credit utilisation.
  • Avoid unnecessary loan enquiries.

A healthy repayment history over several months can significantly improve future loan approvals.

9. Document Verification Failure

Document verification is one of the final stages of loan processing. If the submitted documents don't match the information provided in the application, the bank may reject the request.

Documents commonly verified

  • PAN Card
  • Aadhaar Card
  • Salary Slips
  • Bank Statements
  • Income Tax Returns
  • Address Proof
  • Employment Proof

Common reasons for verification failure include:

  • Edited salary slips
  • Fake employment documents
  • Different signatures
  • Name mismatch across documents
  • Invalid address proof

Always submit genuine and updated documents to avoid unnecessary delays or rejection.

10. Bank-Specific Internal Policies

Sometimes your application may be rejected even if you satisfy the general eligibility requirements.

This happens because every bank has its own internal credit policy that isn't publicly disclosed.

Examples of internal policies

  • Minimum salary requirement
  • Employer category restrictions
  • Industry risk classification
  • Location eligibility
  • Maximum loan exposure limits
  • Existing relationship with the bank

For example, one bank may approve applicants working for a startup, while another may approve loans only for employees of listed companies or government organizations.

Similarly, some lenders avoid financing applicants from specific industries or geographical locations based on their internal risk assessment.

The good news is that most personal loan rejections are avoidable. By improving your credit profile, reducing existing liabilities, maintaining accurate documentation, and applying with the right lender, you can significantly increase your approval chances.

How Banks Evaluate a Personal Loan Application

Many borrowers believe banks approve loans based only on salary or CIBIL score. In reality, lenders use multiple checks to determine whether an applicant can comfortably repay the loan without financial stress.

Understanding this evaluation process helps you prepare a stronger application and significantly improves your chances of approval.

Step 1: Identity & KYC Verification

The first step is verifying your identity using official KYC documents.

Banks typically verify:

  • PAN Card
  • Aadhaar Card
  • Passport (where applicable)
  • Driving Licence
  • Voter ID

Any mismatch in your name, date of birth, address, or PAN details may delay or even reject your application.

Step 2: Income Verification

After identity verification, lenders verify whether your declared income matches your actual earnings.

For Salaried Applicants

  • Latest salary slips
  • Salary account statements
  • Employer verification
  • Form 16 (if required)

For Self-Employed Applicants

  • Income Tax Returns (ITR)
  • GST Returns
  • Business Bank Statements
  • Profit & Loss Statement
  • Business Continuity Proof

Banks also evaluate whether your income has remained stable over time.

Step 3: Credit Bureau Check

Banks obtain your credit report from credit bureaus to understand your repayment history.

They review:

  • CIBIL Score
  • Past loan repayment history
  • Credit card usage
  • Loan enquiries
  • Settled or written-off accounts
  • Current outstanding loans

A good credit history demonstrates responsible borrowing behaviour and increases approval chances.

Step 4: FOIR Calculation

Next, banks calculate your Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR) to determine whether you can comfortably manage another EMI.

Applicants with lower FOIR generally qualify for:

  • Higher loan amounts
  • Lower interest rates
  • Faster approvals

Step 5: Employment & Employer Verification

Banks verify whether you work for a genuine organization and whether your employment is stable.

Some lenders even maintain internal employer rankings.

Applicants working with reputed companies often receive:

  • Higher eligibility
  • Lower interest rates
  • Instant approvals

Step 6: Final Risk Assessment

Finally, all information is combined into a risk profile.

10 Ways to Improve Your Personal Loan Approval Chances

If your previous application was rejected, don't worry. Most borrowers can improve their eligibility by making a few financial improvements before reapplying.

  1. Improve your CIBIL score above 750.
  2. Pay all EMIs and credit card bills on time.
  3. Reduce your credit card utilisation below 30%.
  4. Close unnecessary personal loans.
  5. Avoid applying to multiple banks simultaneously.
  6. Check your credit report for errors.
  7. Submit accurate income and employment details.
  8. Maintain a stable job before applying.
  9. Apply only for the loan amount you actually qualify for.
  10. Take professional guidance if you're unsure about eligibility.

Final Thoughts

A personal loan rejection doesn't necessarily mean you'll never qualify for a loan. It simply indicates that one or more eligibility criteria weren't met at the time of application.

By improving your credit score, reducing existing financial obligations, maintaining stable employment, and applying with the right lender, you can significantly improve your chances of approval.

Instead of applying blindly to multiple banks, understand your eligibility first and choose the lender that best matches your financial profile.
