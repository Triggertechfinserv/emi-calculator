What is FOIR in Personal Loan Eligibility?

FOIR stands for Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio. It is a commonly used measure for assessing a borrower's repayment capacity. FOIR compares a person's fixed monthly financial obligations with their monthly income.

FOIR Formula
FOIR = (Existing Monthly Obligations + Proposed EMI) ÷ Net Monthly Income × 100

For example, if your monthly income is ₹50,000 and your existing monthly obligations are ₹10,000, your existing obligations represent 20% of your monthly income. A lender may then consider its applicable FOIR limit when determining how much additional EMI you can afford.

Why is FOIR Important for Personal Loan Eligibility?

FOIR helps lenders estimate whether a borrower has sufficient monthly income to manage a new loan repayment along with existing financial commitments. A higher level of existing obligations may reduce the maximum EMI available for a new personal loan.

The applicable FOIR can vary between lenders and may depend on factors such as income, customer profile, employment details, existing obligations and the lender's internal credit policy.

How FOIR Affects Your Loan Eligibility

In general, when existing monthly obligations are higher, the amount available for a new EMI may be lower. When monthly obligations are lower relative to income, the borrower may have greater repayment capacity. This is why FOIR is an important factor in personal loan eligibility calculations.
